# Caveats and open questions

Read this before trusting any output of the framework.

## Scientific caveats

1. **No human connectome exists.** Every circuit-level claim about human intuition is inferred from fly, worm, mouse, monkey, lesion patients, surgical single-unit recordings, or fMRI. The fly result (wiring → behavior with no learning) is the strongest *existence proof* that intuition can be feed-forward wiring, but humans add learned modulators at every layer.
2. **fMRI tells you where, not how.** Knutson, Plassmann, and the attraction studies have N between 20 and 30. Direction of effect is probably right; magnitudes are not reliable.
3. **Several popular pillars are shaky.** The somatic marker hypothesis as "the gut decides" is contested. The unconscious thought advantage failed replication. Human habit induction by overtraining has mixed replications. The amygdala low road in humans is debated. Fixed loss-aversion coefficients are not supported. The framework uses the robust parts of each (vmPFC lesions impair real-world decisions; replay happens; habits exist; amygdala tracks negative evaluation; losses often loom larger) and tags the rest tier C.
4. **Panksepp's systems are a taxonomy, not a discovery of eight boxes.** They overlap, interact, and the human evidence is indirect. Barrett's constructionism and Panksepp's basic-affect view are genuinely in tension; this framework uses Panksepp for *drives* (what the body is pushed toward) and Barrett for *feelings* (how the state gets named), which is a pragmatic truce, not a resolution.
5. **Evolutionary explanations are easy to tell and hard to test.** Error management theory makes directional predictions that have replicated; most "just-so" stories do not. Only use the asymmetry question when the two error costs are clearly unequal.
6. **The narrator is not always wrong.** Choice-blindness critics note the choices were trivial. For high-stakes, deliberated decisions, the stated reason may be a real cause. The protocol flags narration; it does not prove it.
7. **Dual-process theory is contested.** Single-process and continuum accounts exist. The framework uses "default-interventionist" as a modeling convenience, not a claim about two literal systems.

## Ethical caveats

- The same analysis that explains a purchase can be used to engineer one. The framework is for understanding and, where used on others, for the explicit goal of helping them; it should not be turned into a manipulation playbook. Phase 4 tooling should refuse prompts that ask for cue design to induce behavior against a target's interest.
- Reconstructing someone's "real" motive from a model is a hypothesis about a person. Present it as such, with confidence levels, and never as a verdict on character. The correspondence-bias literature is a warning aimed at the user of this framework as much as at its subjects.
- Attributions involving sex, status, and disgust are sensitive. Report them when the evidence supports them, in neutral language, with the alternative reconstructions alongside.

## Open questions worth real work

- **Arbitration.** How does the brain decide when deliberation intervenes? Uncertainty-based competition is the leading model but the arbitration circuit is not pinned down.
- **Interoceptive accuracy.** Objective measures of interoception do not correlate well with each other. "Good gut feeling" as a trait is not yet measurable.
- **Individual variation.** Disgust sensitivity, reward sensitivity, and interoceptive awareness vary widely. The framework currently has no person-specific parameters.
- **Culture.** Emotion vocabularies, status signals, and food norms vary; the drives and the valence/arousal core do not. The boundary needs mapping per domain.
- **From wiring to prior.** MICrONS shows like-to-like connectivity; nobody has yet shown a specific human prior living in a specific connectivity pattern.
