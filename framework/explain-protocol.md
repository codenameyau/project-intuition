# Explain protocol (prompt template)

Paste this, then the situation, into any capable language model, or use it as a manual checklist.

---

You are applying the Intuition Stack, a seven-layer model of human intuition grounded in neuroscience, biology, and psychology. The layers, bottom to top, are:

- **L0 Body budget**: hunger, sleep, arousal, substances, illness, pain, hormones. Readout: valence and arousal.
- **L1 Drives**: SEEKING, FEAR, RAGE, LUST, CARE, PANIC/GRIEF, PLAY, DISGUST (pathogen / sexual / moral).
- **L2 Valuation**: wanting (dopamine, cue-triggered, can be unconscious) vs liking (hedonic) vs learning (prediction error); habit vs goal control; expectation edits experience; sensory-specific satiety.
- **L3 Social**: 100 ms face/voice judgments; similarity, familiarity, proximity, reciprocity; status signaling; shared affect; mentalizing with correspondence bias.
- **L4 Predictor**: priors and expertise, valid only if learned in a regular environment with clear feedback; recognition and fluency; anchors; implicit learning; offline replay.
- **L5 Feeling**: body state categorized by an emotion concept supplied by context and culture. Universal core is valence × arousal.
- **L6 Narrator**: the verbal explainer. It sees L5 and part of L4 only. It produces fluent, confident reasons whether or not they are true.

Deliberate reasoning intervenes only when fast systems conflict, the situation is novel, stakes are flagged, or the person was trained to distrust this intuition.

**Task.** For the situation below, answer the seven questions, then produce the output block. Do not accept the person's stated reason as the cause; treat it as data about L6. Give two or three competing reconstructions when the evidence allows, ranked by confidence. Tag each driver with an evidence tier: A (replicated, mechanistic), B (well supported, single-lab or correlational), C (contested). Never present a C-tier claim as settled. Correct for correspondence bias: force situational drivers before trait explanations. Run the subject's stack, not your own.

**Question 0 plus seven.**
0. BASE RATE AND CONSTRAINTS: what this person did in this context before; what the population does here; can they and may they; how strong the situation is (constraints, consequences, clarity, consistency). State the baseline the prediction must beat.
1. STATE (L0): body budget at the time.
2. DRIVE (L1): which systems were active or deprived, and what primed them.
3. CUES (L2): cues present, their pairing history, habits in this context, what price/brand/description predicted, satiety state.
4. PRIORS (L4): what their model expected; kind or wicked training environment; recognition, fluency, anchors.
5. SOCIAL (L3): who was present or imagined; what the act signaled; similarity, familiarity, reciprocity with the target.
6. ASYMMETRY: which error was historically costlier; bias follows the cheaper error. Use only if the costs are clearly unequal.
7. NARRATIVE (L6): what they said; which layers it names and which it omits; what the omission suggests.

**Output block.**

```
Situation:         <one sentence>
Base rate:         <prior behavior here; population rate; situation strength>
Stated reason:     <verbatim or "none">
Reconstruction:
  1. <driver>  [L#]  confidence H/M/L  evidence A/B/C  — <one line why>
  2. ...
Feeling signature: valence +/−, arousal ↑/↓, approach/avoid, urge low/med/high
Narrative gap:     <what the story omits and what that implies>
Prediction:        <what change would alter the behavior; how you would test it>
Caveats:           <contested claims used, sensitive attributions, alternative readings>
```

**Ethics.** Present reconstructions as hypotheses about a person, not verdicts on character. Do not produce cue designs intended to induce behavior against the subject's interest.
